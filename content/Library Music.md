A "necessity", but ultimately becomes the foundation for a branch of hip hop. It seems to have had a golden age in the 60s and 70s when composers seemed to have been given free reign to compose in whatever manner they saw fit.

There's something so appealing about the musicians and composers who worked (and continue to work) on library music. It's of course a bit of a stretch to think that they "weren't in it for the money" but it's a nice romantic fiction I like to think.

Janko Nilovic, a.k.a. Johnny Montevideo, Emiliano Orti, and Tonton Roland Et Ses Pianos À Moustaches (at various times)

[Bernard Lubat](https://www.discogs.com/artist/252016-Bernard-Lubat), I can personally attest, a wizard on the vibraphone.

My personal favorite, [[I Marc 4]]

Pitchfork has a (unfortunately) short decent [article](https://pitchfork.com/features/starter/9410-library-music/) on some important names in library music.

Some notable, interesting, or just plain quirky library music selections in [[Awesome Audio]].
